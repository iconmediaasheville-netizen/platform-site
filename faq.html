Questions Photographers Ask Before Choosing a Platform

A reference for talking with sports and event photographers who are comparing this platform against Pixieset, ShootProof, SmugMug, Zenfolio, and dedicated bib-tagging add-ons like RaceTagger or TagMyRun.

Cost

What's this going to cost me?
Pricing during the pilot is free — no monthly fee, and no commission on sales. Longer-term, the model is a flat rate rather than the tiered, feature-gated pricing the big gallery platforms use, so there's no ladder of plans to climb as the business grows.
Will my bill go up as my photo library grows?
No. Pixieset gates price by storage (10GB up to unlimited, $10–$50/mo) and ShootProof gates it by photo count (1,500 up to 25,000 photos, $10–$30/mo, plus archive fees below their Unlimited tier). This platform offloads full-resolution originals to cloud storage automatically in the background — invisible to the customer, no forced upgrade, no "you've hit your limit" moment mid-season.
Do you take a cut of my sales?
No. Sales run through your own Stripe account directly — the platform never touches a transaction. Worth being fair here: Pixieset and SmugMug are also commission-free on their paid tiers, so this isn't unique to us, but it's a real point of contrast against marketplace-style competitors like FlashFrame, where the pricing model is transaction-based.

Finding photos

How do customers find their own photos in a gallery of thousands?
Every photo is automatically scanned for a bib number the moment it's uploaded and tagged for search — no separate app, no manual tagging, no extra subscription. This is worth pointing out explicitly: tools like TagMyRun, RaceTagger, TrackAction.AI, and Tagily exist specifically because platforms like SmugMug, Zenfolio, and PhotoShelter don't do bib detection natively. A photographer on one of those platforms is, by definition, already paying for or manually running a second tool just to get this. Here it's built in from the start.
What if a customer doesn't know their bib number?
They can upload a selfie and get matched to their own photos by face, powered by AWS Rekognition. None of the major general-purpose platforms (Pixieset, ShootProof, SmugMug, Zenfolio) offer this natively.
Can customers search for a whole team or family at once?
Yes — several bib numbers can be searched at once, separated by commas, and the results can be shared as a single link. Handy for relay teams, families, or a coach checking on a whole roster.

Race-specific features

Can I sell photos with a sponsor or event logo on them?
Yes, automatically — a sponsor or event logo can be composited onto photos at full resolution, with the customer choosing to buy with or without the logo. This is a genuinely race-specific need (title sponsors, timing companies, local business sponsors) that general photo-gallery platforms have no concept of.
How do you handle print orders and shipping?
Saved print-lab profiles (name, address, logo) can be picked per order at fulfillment time, and that lab's exact address is what shows up on the customer's shipping confirmation email — not a generic site address. Tracking numbers and carrier info are captured, and the site flags the moment a print sells so it doesn't get missed.

Uploading & reliability

What happens if my internet drops mid-upload of a few thousand race photos?
The desktop uploader app resumes automatically right where it left off — even after closing the app or restarting the computer — and safely skips anything that already made it through, so nothing gets duplicated. This matters in practice: outdoor race venues on spotty wifi are exactly where a normal browser upload is most likely to fail partway through.
What if a customer searches before their photos are uploaded yet?
They can leave their email and bib number for a "notify me" alert, and the site emails them automatically the moment a matching photo goes up — even days later.
What happens if someone starts checkout and doesn't finish?
An automated reminder email goes out with a one-click link that restores their exact cart. Standard e-commerce practice, but easy to underestimate and not something every small photography platform bothers to build.

Trust & maturity

Is this a marketplace where I'm competing with other photographers for visibility?
No. Each customer gets their own standalone site — their own domain, their own Stripe account, their own admin login. It's not a listing inside someone else's marketplace, so there's no dependency on a third party's search ranking, marketplace fees, or policy changes to reach buyers.
Is this a mature, established platform?
Being straightforward about this, since overselling erodes trust faster than an honest gap does: this is a newer, single-photographer-proven product, not a decade-old platform with a large support team, mobile app, or built-in marketplace traffic. Each pilot customer currently runs on an individually managed server rather than a fully self-serve sign-up product — a real trade-off (more hands-on setup) in exchange for the all-in-one feature set and no storage/photo-count cost ladder. That gap closes as the platform matures, but today's version is early-stage, not a finished competitor to companies that have been public for over a decade.
What kind of support do I get?
Direct access to the person who actually built the platform, rather than a ticket into a large company's support queue. Setup, changes, and troubleshooting go straight to Steve.
Can I give an assistant or second shooter their own login?
Yes — staff accounts with role-based permissions. Staff can do everything day-to-day except touch branding, print labs, or add other users; full admins can do everything.

The bottom line

The pitch isn't any single feature — it's that a general gallery platform plus a separate bib-tagging tool plus a manual print-fulfillment process is what most race and event photographers are actually stitching together today. This is that entire stack, built as one product, with a photographer's actual workflow (bib search, selfie search, sponsor logos, print-lab shipping accuracy) designed in from the start rather than bolted on.
